# A Linguist’s Autopsy of an AI-Generated Text

This case study aims to provide a lingustic commentary on a blog post created by the Google Gemini 2.5 Pro LLM. 

Prompt used: Please write me a typical blog post about the benefits of using emulation to run software on old hardware as opposed to actually running the software on old computers.

The purpose of this case study is to analyse four key areas - Linguistic Accuracy, Factual Correctness, Coherence, and Appropriateness of Tone.

```This is rather factually incorrect. Emulators have existed for a very long time on home and business computers: the first Commodore Amiga 1000 released in 1985 included an MS-DOS emulator, capable of running the 'killer app' Lotus 1-2-3. ```

While the romance of using original hardware is undeniable, the logistical and financial realities can be a significant barrier. Emulation, the process of using modern software to mimic the hardware of older computers, offers a gateway to the past that is more accessible, reliable, and even enriching than wrestling with aging technology.

```While the logistical and financial realities are a barrier, it's important to note that modern software does not necessarily mimic the hardware of older computers. Most emulators are not deterministic or cycle-accurate, which can lead to numerous problems with mission-critical applications. For example, calculations may be carried out incorrectly, the software may not produce the intended results, or it may simply not be compatible with the software that runs on vintage hardware. The choice of the word 'mimic' is unfortunate, as this implies that modern emulators are identical to the vintage hardware.
